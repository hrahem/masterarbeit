What was completed

  • Pseudonymized all 10 source CSVs and generated a deduplicated combined event table.
  • Replaced addresses, recipient-specific subject names, message/event identifiers, and URLs. Removed sender/contact details, geographic fields, user agents, and identifying free text.
  • Grouped event records into one row per eligible send, mapped A/B variants and later rollouts, and computed seven-day outcomes and pre-send history.
  • Compared active delivery and unique-click totals with all nine distinct campaign dashboards: both reconcile exactly.
  • Extracted content templates and simple HTML features from all 20 supplied email files.

This is pseudonymized research data. It preserves cross-campaign linkage and exact source timestamps. Original screenshots/HTML are not included because they may expose personal information. No lookup table or key is exported.

Findings that change the analysis

  1. One duplicate: dach_de_plus_m_consistency26m repeats all 500 events from dach_de_plus_m_consistency26m_3. Only the latter enters combined analysis.
  2. Recent outcomes are incomplete: 7,495 delivered messages in the September 18 campaign have not completed seven days. Their seven-day labels are blank. Re-export on September 26 or later.
  3. Hidden identities: 4,189 delivered messages have no linkable recipient address. Each gets a message-scoped unknown ID, rather than being merged into one fictitious person.
  4. Limited history: 1,436 known recipient IDs occur in multiple campaigns. Only 1,750 primary-pilot rows have a previous delivery observed in these exports. Missing history is not evidence of inactivity.
  5. Limited independent content: the primary pilot covers seven campaigns but only five broad content groups. Three audience-specific March newsletters share content. A/B labels are local and cannot be pooled as universal designs.
  6. Legacy tracking: the May 2023 campaign has no bot filtering. It is retained for secondary analysis and excluded from the primary pilot.
  7. Residual discrepancies: some open totals differ by 1–3 messages; active SENT totals still differ by 16 in the 2023 campaign and by 1 in September 2026. No outcomes were invented to force a match.

What to do next

  1. Start the pilot: use this baseline and implement Beta-Bernoulli Thompson Sampling at realistic campaign sizes. Evaluate controlled scenarios before relying on a fitted simulator.
  2. Collect better coverage: add recent varied campaigns and stable company-generated pseudonymous contact IDs/context. Add full histories where possible.
  3. Validate by campaign and content family: keep related newsletters together and respect chronology. A random row split will overstate generalization.
  4. Train a small predictor only after defining the split: compare a constant probability baseline with regularized logistic regression. Measure probability calibration and uncertainty. Current clicks are sparse and campaign diversity is low.
  5. Delay full RL: the export does not include a complete decision/action/transition record for recipient journeys.

No predictive model or simulator was fitted in this preparation step. No causal uplift or statistically established winner is claimed.
